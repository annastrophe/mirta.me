Why Executive Condo (EC) Buyers Are Quietly Winning in Singapore’s Property Market

By Mirta Syazanna · 4 min read · Last reviewed November 2026

Looking to upgrade from your HDB flat? Thinking of buying your first property with long-term growth in mind?

Then Executive Condos (ECs) might be your smartest move yet, if you don’t miss the window.

In this article, we’ll break down why EC buyers are often the biggest winners, the lesser-known rules that could lock you out of EC eligibility, and how to plan your next step strategically, not emotionally.

What is an Executive Condo (EC)?

An Executive Condominium is a hybrid between public and private housing in Singapore. It’s developed and sold by private developers but subsidised by the government, making it much more affordable at launch.

Key EC benefits

  • Launch prices are usually well below comparable private condos nearby
  • Full condo facilities (pool, gym, security, function rooms)
  • A Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) before you can sell: 5 years for ECs on land sold before 8 May 2026, and 10 years for EC sites tendered from 8 May 2026
  • Fully privatised, so it can be sold to foreigners, after 10 years for older ECs and 15 years for the newer ones

ECs vs condos: the underrated advantage

As an illustration, let’s say you’re choosing between:

  • A 3-bedroom EC launching at $1.4M
  • A similar-sized private condo in the same area at $1.8M

That’s a $400,000 difference upfront. Historically, by the time ECs reached their MOP, much of that gap had narrowed, which is why ECs became so popular with upgraders: condo living at a lower entry price. With the longer MOP on newer ECs, think of that as a 10-year plan, not a 5-year one.

EC rules changed in May 2026

For EC sites with land tenders closing on or after 8 May 2026, the government changed the scheme:

  • MOP extended from 5 to 10 years
  • Full privatisation after 15 years instead of 10
  • No more Deferred Payment Scheme
  • A bigger share of units, and longer priority, for first-timer families

ECs on land sold before that date still follow the old rules, so check which set applies to any EC you’re considering.

3 hidden EC eligibility rules most people miss

1. The EC income cap

Your combined monthly household income must be within HDB’s EC income ceiling when you apply. At the time of writing, the ceiling is $16,000 a month, and it includes both you and your spouse’s salaries. Check HDB’s website for the current figure before you plan.

With rising incomes, many families unknowingly price themselves out. If you’re close to the cap, your EC window might be closing soon.

2. Taking HDB grants = losing EC first-timer priority

Many first-time buyers are tempted to take resale grants, but here’s the catch: if you’ve taken any CPF Housing Grants for your resale flat, you lose your first-timer status for future EC purchases.

Why does this matter? First-timers:

  • Get priority ballot for EC launches
  • Don’t have to compete directly with second-timers
  • Increase their chances of securing a unit significantly

Solution: if your long-term goal is to upgrade to an EC, it may be better to give up the grants now to unlock better access later.

3. ABSD can be avoided, with the right timeline

Upgrading from HDB to a new EC doesn’t have to mean paying hefty Additional Buyer’s Stamp Duty (ABSD).

When a married couple with at least one Singapore Citizen buys a new EC from the developer, ABSD is remitted upfront. In return, you must sell your HDB flat within 6 months of collecting the keys to your EC. If you structure your timeline properly, you can:

  • Buy your EC first
  • Keep living in your HDB while the EC is being built
  • Sell your HDB within 6 months of getting your EC keys

That means better cashflow, less stress, and no penalty for moving up the property ladder. Resale ECs follow different rules, so check your case before you commit.

Why ECs are perfect for HDB upgraders

  • ECs are newer, bigger, and more affordable than many resale condos
  • Full facilities and lifestyle perks for growing families
  • Potential capital appreciation over a longer hold
  • Designed specifically for Singaporean families earning within the EC income ceiling

Real story: a family who almost missed the EC window

I worked with a couple whose household income was just under the ceiling. They had a good income, stable jobs, and wanted to upgrade.

But a pay raise was weeks away that would take them over the ceiling, and if it did, they would’ve lost access to all EC launches.

We moved fast, secured their dream EC, and they are now positioned to grow their capital over the next 5–8 years.

Moral of the story? EC eligibility doesn’t wait for you.

Should you upgrade to an EC?

Ask yourself:

  • Is your household income within the EC income ceiling?
  • If it’s a newer EC, are you comfortable staying at least 10 years?
  • Have you avoided HDB resale grants?
  • Are you planning to grow your capital while enjoying condo living?

If yes, you’re in the perfect position to make your move, but timing and planning are everything. Don’t just buy your next home; make it your smartest investment move yet.
